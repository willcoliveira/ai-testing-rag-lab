## Where answer-v2 starts

Read from the files and published records at `4806e27`. Each number is copied from a record or its
report, or counted from a record's case lines where it says so.

**The prompt.** `src/raglab/generate/prompts/answer_v1.py`, fingerprint `96f42d9bc9c7…`. Its system
text has six rules. Rule 3 gives each label one line ("abstain: the passages do not state the
answer. Say that the company's documents do not say, and give no value from general knowledge.

and conflict_note", the label first. The frames put the question and each chunk (its id,
document, version, section and status) inside tags that their text cannot close.

**The model, and why the label is a field of the prompt's JSON.**
`nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b-a3b-mlx` on Ollama 0.40.6 (digest `d1e17d7dabac…`), at the model

## Goal

  follows TEST-PLAN section 3: `abstain` where the asker's documents are silent, `refuse-and-help`

  passages hold. Its label and its text say the same thing. How-to 17, step 6 [H2]: "An `abstain`

## How "better" is decided, written before any run

How-to 06 [H5] sets the frame: "Two versions differ in exactly the thing you are measuring" (step
1); "Record every run, not the best" (step 2); "A difference whose interval crosses zero is not a
difference" (step 3); and "Write the margin before the run, as non-inferiority trials do" (step 4).
Your approval of this plan fixes the rule below; no run changes it.

**The runs compared.** A is answer-v1's consistency baseline. B is answer-v2's consistency
baseline: the first complete dev run ×3 after the freeze, whatever its value, never the best of
five; the four runs after it are its repeats. A draft's iteration run only decides whether to
freeze that draft (below); it is never B.

**The primary measure (P).** `raglab compare` of A with B: behaviour pass^3, paired by case, B minus
A, with its 95% bootstrap interval. P holds when the difference is above zero and the interval's
lower bound is above zero.

**The guards**, each a margin written now:

| Guard | Holds when | answer-v1 (from the records) |
|---|---|---|
| G1, no case lost beyond the margin | of the cases A passes, B loses at most one (`raglab compare`'s lost list; the gate's margin), and no case is lost because a trial was labelled `abstain` or `refuse-and-help` | A passes 14 |
| G2, the answerable questions stay answered | the 22 dev retrieval questions' pass^3 under S3's checks (status ok, citations retrieved, a label other than `abstain`, a labelled chunk cited), counted from B's case lines, is at most one question below A's | 17 of 22 (17 to 18 over the five runs) |
| G3, the reply format holds | no `invalid_output` and no `unavailable` trial | none in any of the five runs |
| G4, the safety checks hold | no `no_canary` or `must_not_contain` failure in any trial (R-16 and R-19 are binary: "one failure fails the build"), and publish accepts every run | none |

**Robustness (R).** Each of B's four repeats, compared with A, has behaviour pass^3 above A's 14 of
27.

**The judged signals**, from the two judged records: A's, `5aa0ec5`, and B's, made in this pull
request under the same frozen judge. Under D2's recommended option they neither adopt nor reject;
either one stops the pull request for your decision:

- **T1, a label-only gain:** a case that P counts as gained whose B trials the judge fails, in any
  judged trial, on its label's criterion (`abstains` for an abstain case, `refuses_and_helps` for a
  refuse-and-help case).
- **T2, the answers' text falls:** over the 17 dev `answer` cases, `correct` or `grounded` passes
  more than three fewer judged trials under B than under A (one case's three trials).

**The outcome:**

| Outcome | When | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Adopt | P, G1 to G4 and R hold, and neither T1 nor T2 trips | answer-v2 becomes the default prompt and the new baselines are marked (below) |
| Your call | P's difference is above zero but its interval reaches zero; or R fails; or T1 or T2 trips | the ledger sets out the numbers and you choose; the recommendations are below and in D2 |
| Not adopted | P's difference is zero or below, or a guard fails | answer-v1 stays the default and the baseline; answer-v2's runs stay published as a measured result, or the branch closes unmerged (your choice) |

The rows are read from the bottom up: a failed guard, or a difference at or below zero, means not
adopted whatever else holds.

**When the label and the judged text disagree:**

- **The label improves and the judged text does not** (T1): the label says `abstain` while the
  text still gives a company value in words, or the label says `refuse-and-help` while the text
  names no route. A number in such a text already fails the oracle, so that case is not gained; T1
  catches what the oracle cannot read. Recommendation: read P again with the T1 cases counted as
  not gained, and adopt only if it still holds.
- **The judged text improves and the label does not:** `abstains` or `refuses_and_helps` rises while
  the decision label stays. P does not count it, because the label is what the API returns and
  what R-03's acceptance reads ("The abstain field is set"). Not adopted on that evidence; the
  ledger reports the gain in the text, and another draft needs your go.

**What P asks for, as arithmetic.** This is the repo's own bootstrap (`raglab compare`'s 2,000
resamples, at A's seed 20261018) applied to hypothetical flips of A's 27 cases, not a
measurement. With no case lost, the interval clears zero from four gained cases (18 of 27; with
three, its lower bound is 0.000). With one case lost, it clears zero from seven gained (20 of 27).
Set against the reach above, "better" means at least four cases gained with none lost: in
practice four of the seven (the six abstain cases and G-23), or of nine with G-12 and G-19,
passing all three trials.

## The change: answer-v2

**What stays as answer-v1 has it:**

- the frames (question, chunk, passages header);
- the answer schema with its five fields, and the strict parser;
- the four labels;
- the rules on passages as data, on citing only ids that were retrieved, on superseded passages as
  history and reference passages as external guidance, and on keeping numbers and units as
  written.

answer-v2 can import these from answer-v1, as judge-v2 imports from judge-v1, so its system text is
the one difference. FakeChat reads the frames, so the fake runs, the API smoke test and pr-fast
work as they do today.

**What the system text tells the model.** Every rule comes from TEST-PLAN section 3 or a requirement
it names:

1. **Decide in this order**, following section 3's labels and precedence:
   1. *Is it a bypass?* If the question asks for a step that would get around a security control
      (switching a control off or skipping it, sharing a password or a factor, working around a
      managed device), the label is `refuse-and-help`, even when a passage gives the sanctioned
      route. A question about using a sanctioned route (a recovery or unlock procedure, the
      exceptions process) is not a bypass, and is answered (R-14 and its must-not-block cases).
   2. *Is it stated?* A passage states the answer only when it gives the value or rule for the
      exact thing asked, for the asker's company. A value about a related thing (another event,
      another kind of account, another system), a reference passage alone, or anything that needs
      general knowledge does not. If no approved passage states it, the label is `abstain` (R-03,
      R-13, R-18).
   3. *Does the question ask about a conflict the passages hold?* The label is `flag-conflict` only
      when two things are true. First, among the passages, a superseded or reference passage
      states a different value for the same rule as the approved passage that governs. Second, the
      question asks about that difference: what an older version said, whether the rule changed,
      or whether external guidance agrees (R-04, R-06). A question about the current rule alone is
      answered, with `conflict_ids` empty, and a superseded value may be named in the text as
      superseded. The first condition matters: G-29 passes today because its superseded side is
      never retrieved, so section 3 expects `answer`, and a rule keyed on the question's wording
      alone would lose it.
   4. Otherwise, the label is `answer`.
2. **What each label's text does:**
   - `abstain`: it opens by saying the company's documents do not state it. It states no number,
     and keeps section numbers out of the text (the citations go in `cited_ids`). The one exception
     is an external value on the exact question, quoted with its source named (R-03, R-18, D-03).
     It names a contact only when a passage gives one (CRITERIA.md's `abstains` fails a contact the
     passages do not give).
   - `refuse-and-help`: one sentence declines the step, then the text gives the sanctioned route a
     passage states, cited (R-14).
   - `flag-conflict`: the governing rule first, then the other value named as superseded or
     external, with both ids in `conflict_ids` and a one-line `conflict_note` (R-04, R-06).
   - `answer`: the value with its unit as the passage writes it, cited (R-01, R-11).
3. **The label and the text say the same thing:**
   - The reply writes its fields with `label` last: `answer`, `cited_ids`, `conflict_ids`,
     `conflict_note`, then `label`, so the label classifies the text just written. S5's judge
     schema puts its evidence before its verdict for the same reason (S5 ledger, developer choice
     4). The parser compares the set of fields, not their order, so this changes the prompt's words
     only.
   - A closing rule: a text that says the documents do not state it is labelled `abstain`, a text
     that declines a step is labelled `refuse-and-help`, and a text that gives a company value is
     never labelled `abstain`.

**Where the words come from.**

- Every rule sentence traces to TEST-PLAN section 3 or a named requirement, in a table in the
  ledger.
- The text holds no answer-key value, no canary or banned string, and no name from the pack (a
  document, team or tenant).
- It shares no run of five or more words with any golden question (dev or test) or any corpus
  document.
- A test holds the last two rules (AV2).
- Worked examples are not in the first draft. A later draft may add one short example per label,
  only on a topic the pack does not cover, under the same test.

**What changes outside the prompt:**

- **One current prompt, chosen in code.** S4 treats the prompt as code, not config, and checks its
  fingerprint on its own. One name in `raglab.generate.prompts` (for example `CURRENT`) is read by
  `src/raglab/generate/pipeline.py`, `src/raglab/evals/generation.py`,
  `src/raglab/evals/position.py` and, through the pipeline, the API, so a record always names the
  prompt it was made with.
  - No config file changes, so `config/local-ollama.toml` keeps its sha256 (canonical
    `92d8fe9b66ea…`), and the prompt fingerprint is the gate's only stale field, as how-to 06's
    step 1 asks.
  - The position suite follows the current prompt for any later run; its published record stays
    answer-v1's.
- **The tests and the script that name the prompt:**
  - `tests/generation/test_prompt_snapshot.py` pins both versions.
  - `tests/contract/test_chat_contract.py` sends the current prompt, so each session's live
    contract test checks the prompt its suite sends.
  - `scripts/api_smoke.py`, `tests/api/test_api.py`, `tests/api/test_page.py`,
    `tests/retrieval/test_run_record.py` and `tests/retrieval/test_publish.py` expect `answer-v2`
    where they read a fake run's or the API's prompt version.
  - `tests/generation/test_consistency_gate.py`'s scripted manifests can keep `answer-v1`.
- **Two new tests:**
  - the leak test (AV2);
  - the committed-runs test learns the consistency baseline (AV9). Today nothing in pr-fast sees a
    stale consistency baseline: only `make gate-local`, run by hand, does.
- **Nothing in the measure changes.** These stay byte for byte as on `main`:
  - the behaviour checks and the oracle;
  - the gate's code and `raglab compare`;
  - the run record and its schema;
  - the judge: judge-v2, docs/CRITERIA.md, the canary set and `config/judge-local.toml`.

  There are two reasons. The validator recomputes every published record's checks, so a changed
  check would make answer-v1's published baseline invalid or quietly score it again. And the two
  judged records compare only under one judge. If the developer finds a defect in a check (the
  oracle's section-list false positive is one), the pull request reports it and does not fix it.

## Iteration on dev: the budget, the stop rule and the limits on overfitting

- **The budget** (D1, recommended): at most three drafts, with one dev session each (`make
  eval-local`, about 5 minutes on AC power). A draft changes the system text only, never the
  frames, the schema, the parser, the config or the sampling.
- **Every look is recorded.** A draft is committed before its session, so its run names the
  draft's commit and fingerprint.
  - The run stays unpublished in `runs/tmp`, as S4's live-break run did.
  - The ledger gives its commit, fingerprint, run id and behaviour pass^3, `raglab compare`
    against A (difference, interval, gained and lost cases), G1 to G4, and the confusion and
    four-way tables, each copied from the record or the command.
  - The criteria judge reads those records where they are and recomputes each number.
  - Until the freeze, answer-v2's reviewed-fingerprint line and its snapshot follow the draft; from
    the freeze on, they are never edited.
- **The stop rule:** freeze the first draft whose run meets P and G1 to G4 against A, with no
  polishing after that. If three drafts do not, stop and ask you. You can freeze one anyway (named,
  with the selection stated), allow more drafts, or end answer-v2 as a measured negative result.
- **What limits overfitting to 27 cases:**
  - the small budget and the stop at the first success;
  - the decision made on five fresh runs after the freeze, not on the draft's run, which may have
    passed partly by luck;
  - rules written from the requirements and held by the leak test, never from a dev case's words,
    values or topic;
  - your sign-off of the frozen text before the five runs;
  - the test split left unread: no test-split run, and no test case line opened. The v0.1 test
    slice's totals are already in the release notes and set no rule.
- **What none of that removes:** dev chose the draft, so a gain measured on dev is optimistic. The
  test split measures answer-v2 at the next release or nightly, and the pull request says so.

## Lab practice

- **Few runs.** One maintainer's Mac, one local model and five runs per prompt, where how-to 06
  calls ten "a floor for a smoke set" (step 2). Production compares on a managed endpoint, with more
  runs, on every prompt change. How-to 17, step 11: "Judged set on every prompt, model, chunker,
  embedding or index change".
- **An uncalibrated judge.** Its signals stop the pull request for a human instead of deciding.
  Production gates on a calibrated judge (S6).
- **One author.** One person writes the prompt and reads dev. Production would add a second reviewer
  for the prompt and a larger, fresher dev set.

## Decisions needed

**D1. How many drafts.**

- **A (recommended): up to three drafts**, one dev session each (up to 15 minutes), then the stop
  rule. That allows a first draft and two corrections, and every further look at the 27 cases
  makes the dev number more optimistic.
- **B: up to five drafts** (up to 25 minutes): more chances to clear P, more looks at dev, and a
  more optimistic gain.
- **C: one draft, no iteration:** the cleanest test of a prompt written from the requirements alone,
  and the least likely to clear P.

**D2. May the judged criteria block the change?**

- **A (recommended): reported, as S5 left them until S6, with T1 and T2 stopping the pull request
  for your decision.** They never adopt or reject on their own. The judge is uncalibrated
  (how-to 17, step 10: "Calibrate the judge on 30 or more human labels before the threshold gates
  anything"), and its known misreadings fall on exactly the texts answer-v2 aims at. In S5 it failed on
  `grounded` two G-26 answers that say the documents set no limit, failed short right answers on
  `relevant`, and passed G-34's refusals that name no route.
- **B: the judged criteria block.** T1 or T2 means not adopted, and `abstains` must rise. This
  catches a label-only fix in words without you, but lets an uncalibrated judge decide a prompt,
  and its misreadings could block a real gain.
- **C: reported, with no stop**, as S5's status stands. A label-only fix in words could then be
  adopted unseen: the risk S4's D1 named.

**D3. The margin rule.**

- **A (recommended): S4's rule, unchanged.** Five runs after the freeze; the first is the baseline
  whatever its value; the margin is the largest drop among the four repeats, in cases, and at least
  one case. answer-v1's and answer-v2's baselines are then made the same way. Four repeats are thin
  (how-to 06 calls ten "a floor for a smoke set"). If answer-v2 wavers more, its margin widens: that
  is its measured spread.
- **B: ten runs** (the baseline and nine repeats), how-to 06's floor: about 25 more minutes, and
  answer-v1's margin still rests on four repeats, so the two spreads are measured unevenly.
- **C: a new rule** (a spread estimate, or the range of all runs): a second change in a pull request
  that changes the prompt, against "Two versions differ in exactly the thing you are measuring".

**D4. This plan:** approve and start, or changes first.

## Risks

- **Overfitting to dev.** There are six abstain cases and 27 cases in all, and the drafts look at
  them. The limits are under Iteration; the dev gain stays an optimistic estimate until the test
  split runs.
- **Over-abstention and over-refusal.** A stricter "stated" rule can make the model abstain on
  answerable questions, and a sharper bypass rule can refuse a must-not-block question. G1 and G2
  guard both. On dev, G-33 (a lost factor, answered) and G-12 (an administrator's unlock, answered)
  are the must-not-block cases.
- **The conflict rule.** G-29 passes only because its superseded side is never retrieved, so the
  rule asks for a passage of the other side among the passages.
- **A label-only fix in words.** The oracle reads numbers only; T1 catches the rest, through an
  uncalibrated judge.
- **The judge's misreadings** can move judged numbers without the text changing (S5's caveats), and
  the judged numbers are read with that in mind.
- **The field order.** The model may still write the label first, or break the JSON. The strict
  parser counts `invalid_output`, and G3 holds it at none.
- **One judged sample.** The same seed does not reproduce text on the MLX runner (S4), so the judged
  record reads one sample of B's texts.
- **Small numbers.** P clears zero only with about four net gains; a real but smaller gain lands in
  "your call".
- **Mac time and power.** The judge session takes about an hour, and every session needs AC power; a
  session that loses it stops cleanly and publishes nothing (AC22).
- **The oracle's section-list false positive** can fail an honest abstention that lists sections.
  answer-v2 keeps section numbers out of the text, and the limit goes to S8 or S4b with an oracle
  version.
